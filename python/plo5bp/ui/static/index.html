How Study works
  1. Set the table. Choose Your seat and the number of players above; click any stack to change it, or click a seat for its menu.
  2. Enter the cards. Click cards in the grid — or type a rank then a suit, like As. They fill your hand, then the flops, turns and rivers, in order.
  3. Enter every action in order — each player’s Fold, Check/Call or Bet, yours included. Undo or click a history line to go back.
  4. Read the network’s answer in Recommendation on your turns: its top action, how often it takes each action, and the sizes it likes.

What you get

A trainer, a study tool and a review, built for one format.

Trainer

Random bomb pots dealt instantly, any stack depth, two- to six-handed. Play them against the network and every move is graded — Best move, Correct, Inaccuracy, Wrong, Blunder.

Review

After each hand, step through every decision — yours and theirs — see what each of yours cost in EV, and swap a card to see how the answer changes.

Study

Enter a hand you played: seats, stacks, cards and actions. The network answers at every decision, with the bet sizes it prefers. Share any spot with a link.

Home games

Play with your friends — on the same tables.

Start a private club, send your friends an invite link, and deal double-board PLO5, PLO6 or PLO67 bomb pots on any phone or computer.

  • Private clubs. Only members see your tables, hand histories and numbers.

Why a neural network

Classical solvers can't touch this format. A network can.

Any stack depth

Real games don't come in preset configurations. The model trains across two- to six-handed pots with stacks from short to 300bb deep — and reads the exact stacks in front of it, not the nearest template.

Any bet size

No fixed bet menu. The network converges on its own preferred size for each spot — a continuous choice from min-bet to pot — and grades your sizing against that judgment, not a coarse grid.

Every multiway spot

Bomb pots are multiway by nature, which is exactly where game trees explode. The network doesn't enumerate the tree — it plays the spot in front of it, however many players are in.

What it is — and isn't

Not a true solver. Honestly better than pretending.

WrapGTO does not compute a Nash equilibrium. The model approximates GTO play, learned through self-play — that's a real limitation, and I'd rather state it than market around it.

The alternative doesn't exist: a true CFR solver for double-board PLO5 bomb pots would require crushing abstractions — pre-configured seat and stack layouts, a handful of allowed bet sizes, and heavy card bucketing — before the tree fits in any computer. What you'd gain in theoretical purity, you'd lose in relevance to the hand you actually played.

I'm also building a best-response probe that measures how much a perfect adversary could win against the live model. The number will be published here when it's done — whatever it says.

Why it's free

The models are still in development, so the whole site is free.

The networks behind WrapGTO get stronger with every training run, but they aren't yet at the level I'd be comfortable charging for. Until they are, everything is open: unlimited trainer hands, full Study, home games. New weights reach the site regularly as training goes on.

If paid plans ever arrive, anyone using the site will hear about it well in advance — priced to cover GPU time, not to turn a profit.

Why this exists

A study tool first. A business barely.

WrapGTO started as a personal project: I'm a professional player who wanted to study double-board bomb pots and found that nothing for the format existed — no solver, no trainer, no library. So I built the model, the engine, and this site to study against myself. Each new format is weeks of training at around $50 a day, funded out of my own poker winnings. I'd be running it either way.
